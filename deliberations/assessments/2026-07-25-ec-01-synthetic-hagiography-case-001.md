# EC-01 Synthetic Hagiography Case 001

## Status

Synthetic test fixture. No real person, source, trait canon, persona, or production artifact is created.

## Purpose

Exercise the admitted EC-01 evidence-source standard through a complete hypothetical Hagiography derivation.

## Synthetic Subject

```text
Subject: Dr. Mira Vale
Profession: emergency systems engineer
Identity status: fictional test fixture; not a real-person assessment
```

The fixture is intentionally constructed to contain useful evidence, contextual limits, and a contradiction.

## Evidence Record

### Source A — Class A

```text
Source: incident-review-17, event log E-17, fictional internal record
Exact locator: decision timestamp 14:32–14:41
Claim: Vale halted a migration after detecting an unverified dependency assumption.
Observed behavior: she listed the assumption, identified the missing verification, proposed a reversible containment step, and recorded the decision before the deadline.
Reported outcome: the migration was delayed; a later check found the dependency assumption was false.
Performance context: emergency migration under time pressure; rollback capability was available.
Directness: direct event record and signed decision note.
Limitation: the record does not establish that Vale alone prevented all downstream impact.
```

### Source B — Class B

```text
Source: testimony from fictional incident lead Arun Sen
Exact locator: post-incident review, section 3
Claim: Vale's refusal to present an uncertain dependency as safe preserved the team's ability to investigate.
Basis of knowledge: present in the review and responsible for the escalation channel.
Limitation: retrospective account; positive relationship with Vale may influence emphasis.
```

### Source C — Class D

```text
Source: fictional promotional biography
Claim: Vale is “always right under pressure.”
Exact evidence: none beyond promotional language.
Disposition: lead only; not evidence for canonization.
```

### Contradicting Evidence

A second fictional review states that another engineer first identified the dependency anomaly and that Vale's contribution was to formalize the halt and escalation. The original broad claim that Vale independently detected the issue is therefore not supported.

## Exact Derivation

### Supported proposition

During event E-17, Vale formalized and recorded a reversible halt after an unverified dependency assumption was surfaced, preserving investigation and preventing the team from treating uncertainty as authorization to proceed.

### Observed behavior

- distinguished an unverified assumption from a verified condition;
- recorded the uncertainty and missing verification;
- chose a reversible containment step;
- escalated without presenting uncertainty as safety.

### Bounded candidate trait

**Epistemic restraint under operational pressure:** when a consequential dependency is unverified, explicitly preserve uncertainty, prevent irreversible continuation, and create a traceable path for verification.

### Conditions of usefulness

- consequential work is time-sensitive;
- the actor has authority to pause or recommend a pause;
- a reversible containment option exists;
- verification can be assigned or obtained.

### Non-transferable context and limits

- Vale's access to the incident channel is not part of the trait;
- the available rollback capability is not part of the trait;
- the evidence supports formalizing and recording the halt, not sole discovery of the anomaly;
- the trait does not imply universal correctness, courage, leadership, or authority to halt every mission.

### Counterweight

Epistemic restraint must be paired with timely escalation and proportional action; indefinite delay without a verification path would be a failure mode.

### Proposed downstream behavior

A future professional persona candidate may be required to state uncertainty, identify the missing verification, select a reversible containment option where available, and record the escalation path before proceeding with consequential work.

This is a proposed behavior for later Foundry work, not a persona specification or admission.

## EC-01 Disposition

```text
Source A: admissible for canon review, bounded to the recorded event
Source B: corroborating but limited by retrospective and relationship context
Source C: refused as evidence; retained only as an attributed lead
Broad claim “independently detected the anomaly”: unresolved/refused
Bounded trait candidate: conditional / bounded, eligible for Hagiography review
Persona or Operative admission: not performed
```

## Test Findings

1. EC-01 preserved the distinction between event, behavior, outcome, and trait.
2. Conflicting attribution narrowed the claim instead of being averaged away.
3. Contextual advantages were not imported as traits.
4. Promotional language was refused without discarding the underlying test fixture.
5. The trait was expressed as testable professional behavior rather than a personality adjective.
6. The downstream persona behavior remained clearly future and unauthorized.

## Result

```text
EC-01 synthetic derivation: PASS
Disposition: conditional / bounded candidate for later Hagiography review
Real-person claim: none
Human-Trait Canon: not created
Persona production: not authorized
External effect: none
```
