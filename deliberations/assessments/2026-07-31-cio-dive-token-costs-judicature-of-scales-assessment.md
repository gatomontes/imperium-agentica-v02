# CIO Dive Token-Cost Finding — Judicature of Scales

- Date: 2026-07-31
- Source: [CIO Dive — As token costs mount, leaders revise their AI plans](https://www.ciodive.com/news/AI-spending-management-token-costs/826656/)
- Record class: assessment
- Status: recorded; no semantic admission or implementation claimed

## Confirmed external findings

The article reports an EY survey of 500 U.S.-based senior decision-makers:

- More than four in five organizations investing in AI reported concerns about token usage and implementation cost.
- 37% were expanding planned AI implementations despite those concerns; 15% were narrowing deployment.
- More than two-thirds lacked accurate visibility into AI software use.
- Approximately three in five reported increased AI overspend.
- FinOps is expanding from cloud-cost management toward AI-cost management and token economics.

These are attributed external findings. They are not treated as independently verified Imperium measurements.

## Imperium relevance

AI resource consumption is a governance concern because uncontrolled model, token, compute, retry, and provider use can alter mission risk, feasibility, and value. Resource expenditure must therefore remain distinguishable from mission authority, tool authority, credential authority, execution, and Runtime operation.

The existing Imperium separation of Curia, Muster, Armory, Locksmith, Execution, provenance, evidence, and Runtime provides relevant attachment points, but it does not by itself establish an economic adjudication function.

## Deliberation

The proposed **Judicature of Scales** is a candidate Imperium function for adjudicating proportionality between mission purpose and cognitive/resource expenditure.

Its candidate scope includes:

1. assessing proposed model, token, compute, retry, and provider consumption against mission purpose and declared constraints;
2. attributing consumption to mission, operative, station, model, provider, and execution attempt;
3. supporting cost-aware model and capability selection;
4. identifying runaway loops, prompt inflation, excessive retries, unauthorized escalation, and unexplained overspend;
5. authorizing or recommending exceptional consumption according to separately established authority;
6. supporting interruption or return when resource thresholds are reached;
7. reconciling expenditure with mission outcomes without treating output volume as mission value;
8. preserving an economic record linked to provenance, evidence, and mission disposition.

## Boundary

The Judicature of Scales is not yet an admitted office, layer, Runtime mechanism, billing system, or provider authority.

This assessment does not authorize:

- live model calls;
- provider interaction;
- credentials;
- deployment;
- interruption behavior;
- budget enforcement;
- a new decision-maker in Curia;
- transfer of authority from Curia, Muster, Armory, Locksmith, Execution, or Master Mason.

The candidate function must not become a covert substitute for mission authority. Its economic judgment may inform or constrain an authorized mission only after its authority, artifacts, evidence, and enforcement boundaries are separately defined and admitted.

## Preliminary conclusion

Imperium should treat model and token expenditure as governed mission resources. No operative should receive unlimited cognition merely because a provider permits it.

A future contract should define a resource envelope, cost attribution, threshold behavior, exception handling, value/outcome linkage, and post-mission reconciliation. The **Judicature of Scales** is recorded as the candidate name for that adjudicative function.

## Open questions

- Is the Judicature of Scales an office, a Curia instrument, a cross-layer contract, or a function distributed among existing institutions?
- Who may authorize an expenditure exception?
- Does it recommend interruption, command interruption, or only report threshold breach?
- What is the authoritative cost record when provider billing and local telemetry disagree?
- How are rapidly changing model prices, capabilities, and provider terms versioned?
- What evidence demonstrates that expenditure was proportionate to mission purpose?

## Residual uncertainty

The external findings are survey-based and article-mediated. Their scope, definitions, sampling, and underlying EY/Flexera methods require separate source review before they support an Imperium risk claim.
