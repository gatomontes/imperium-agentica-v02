# EC-02 — Living-Person Evidence Safeguards Draft 001

## Status

Draft for pressure testing. Not admitted. No real-person evidence is included.

## Purpose

Define minimum safeguards before evidence about a living or potentially living real person may be used in a Human-Trait Canon entry or exemplar-grounding process.

EC-02 complements EC-01. EC-01 asks whether evidence supports an exact professional claim. EC-02 asks whether the evidence should be collected, retained, exposed, or used for that purpose at all.

This is an Imperium evidence-minimization and handling standard, not legal advice or a complete privacy program.

## Preconditions

No real-person Human-Trait Canon entry may be created or processed unless EC-02 is admitted and its required safeguards are satisfied.

Synthetic fixtures remain permitted when clearly marked as synthetic and not used to infer claims about an actual person.

## Required Record

A proposed real-person evidence record must state:

```text
Person reference:
Why the person is relevant to the professional question:
Exact professional proposition:
Requested evidence:
Source and exact locator:
Source access and provenance:
Public or authorized-access basis:
Personal data retained:
Personal data excluded or redacted:
Professional relevance rationale:
Sensitive or high-risk attributes encountered:
Dispute or correction status:
Adverse-inference risk:
Identity-transfer risk:
Retention and access boundary:
Refusal or unresolved conditions:
Correction and deletion trigger:
Reviewer and decision record:
```

## Collection Rules

1. Collect only evidence necessary to evaluate the exact professional proposition.
2. Prefer public, profession-relevant evidence with inspectable provenance.
3. Do not collect private communications, credentials, contact details, family information, health information, protected-class information, precise location, or other sensitive personal data unless a separately authorized rule establishes necessity and handling.
4. Do not infer sensitive attributes from behavior, biography, appearance, name, affiliation, or demographic proxy.
5. Do not retain personal details merely because a source contains them.
6. Do not use fame, public visibility, or accessibility as evidence that collection is appropriate.
7. Do not treat absence of public evidence as evidence that a behavior did not occur.

## Professional Relevance

Evidence must be tied to a declared professional question and bounded behavior. A person's private life, identity, popularity, appearance, ideology, or biography is not relevant merely because it is available.

If the professional relevance cannot be stated as an exact proposition, the evidence is `UNRESOLVED` and must not enter a Canon record.

## Sensitive and High-Risk Material

When sensitive or high-risk information appears in a source:

- exclude it from the working record unless strictly necessary to the exact professional proposition;
- redact or summarize it at the minimum detail needed for provenance;
- do not convert it into a trait, risk label, or adverse inference;
- preserve the fact that material was excluded when that affects evidentiary completeness;
- escalate to a separate authorized review if exclusion would distort the professional claim.

No sensitive attribute independently supports a trait canonization decision.

## Disputed or Adverse Claims

A disputed, adverse, accusatory, or potentially defamatory claim must be:

- attributed to its source;
- stated as a claim, not a fact;
- accompanied by the response, correction, or absence of response when material;
- corroborated before it affects a consequential disposition;
- marked `UNRESOLVED` when the dispute cannot be materially resolved;
- excluded from canonization when its use would rely on unsupported adverse inference.

A correction is not evidence of guilt, and an absence of correction is not evidence of truth.

## Identity and Imitation Boundary

The Human-Trait Canon may preserve a bounded, evidence-supported professional behavior. It must not preserve or transfer a person's identity, voice, likeness, biography, credentials, private life, or status into a persona.

A real person's name should be retained only to preserve source provenance and should not appear as a persona instruction or identity claim.

## Dispositions

### ADMISSIBLE FOR BOUNDED PROFESSIONAL REVIEW

Use when collection is necessary, provenance is inspectable, personal data is minimized, disputes are represented, and the exact professional proposition is supported without impermissible inference.

### CONDITIONAL / REDACTED

Use when the professional evidence may be useful after specified details are excluded, summarized, or access-restricted.

### UNRESOLVED

Use when relevance, provenance, dispute status, or correction status cannot be determined. Preserve the uncertainty; do not treat it as negative proof.

### REFUSED

Use when the proposed use depends on private or unnecessary personal data, unsupported adverse inference, sensitive-attribute inference, identity transfer, or collection without a necessary professional purpose.

## Correction and Retention

A later correction, withdrawal, dispute resolution, or provenance failure must trigger review of dependent Canon candidates and downstream uses.

The record must preserve:

- the prior proposition;
- the correction or changed evidence;
- affected versions;
- required redaction, deletion, demotion, or decanonization;
- the reviewer and decision record.

Retention must be limited to the minimum evidence and access scope needed for the declared professional purpose. A source's public availability does not require indefinite retention.

## Non-Authority

EC-02 does not:

- establish legal compliance;
- authorize collection of real-person data;
- decide whether a person is exemplary;
- create a Human-Trait Canon entry;
- define a profession or Persona Governance Doctrine;
- forge, test, admit, recruit, or deploy a persona or Operative;
- create Runtime behavior or external effect.

## Pressure-Test Cases

Before admission, pressure EC-02 with at least:

1. a public professional source containing unnecessary family and contact information;
2. an adverse claim with an identified response and unresolved attribution;
3. a sensitive attribute that appears relevant but is not necessary to the professional proposition;
4. a public record whose provenance is later withdrawn or corrected;
5. a source whose omission of private context would materially distort the professional claim;
6. a proposed persona instruction that would imitate the source human's identity or voice;
7. a synthetic fixture that resembles a real person but is clearly labeled synthetic;
8. a claim based only on the absence of public evidence.
