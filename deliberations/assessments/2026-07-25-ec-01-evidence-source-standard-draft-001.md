# EC-01 — Evidence-Source Standard Draft 001

## Status

Draft for pressure testing. Not admitted doctrine. Not a persona-production gate. Not a Human-Trait Canon.

## Purpose

Define the minimum evidence discipline required before a real-world exemplar may support an achievement-to-behavior-to-trait derivation for Hagiography or The Gesta.

EC-01 operationalizes DR-007 and DR-009 without treating source count, reputation, rank, fame, or apparent neutrality as truth.

## Scope

EC-01 applies only when evidence about a real-world human is used to support:

```text
demonstrated professional performance, decision, work product, or outcome
→ observed behavior or decision pattern
→ bounded trait
→ proposed professional or Officer behavior
```

It does not evaluate a person's moral worth, diagnose a person, establish universal causation, or authorize persona or Officer production.

## Evidence Chain

The evidence chain must identify a demonstrated professional performance, decision, work product, or outcome. A discrete achievement is not required. A sustained practice, prevented failure, documented refusal, or bounded decision may qualify when the exact event or record and its relevance are inspectable.

## Required Evidence Record

Every proposed exemplar derivation must preserve:

```text

Publication or event date:

Performance or decision context:

Uncertainty:

Falsification or revision condition:

```

A source title, reputation, or link without an exact claim and locator is not sufficient evidence.

## Source Classes

### Class A — Direct or primary evidence

Examples include the person's contemporaneous work product, decision record, public testimony, measured result, official record, or direct observation by an identified observer with relevant access.

Class A evidence may support a claim when the record establishes what occurred, when it occurred, and why the source is connected to the event. It does not by itself prove the trait interpretation or causal contribution.

### Class B — Identified professional or contemporaneous account

Examples include an identified colleague, client, reviewer, investigator, or contemporaneous professional account with relevant access and a stated basis for knowledge.

Class B evidence may support a claim when its scope and incentives are recorded. It should be corroborated when the claim is material, disputed, adverse, or causally strong.

### Class C — Independent secondary synthesis

Examples include reputable reporting, scholarship, case analysis, or institutional review that identifies its sources and permits the underlying claim to be checked.

Class C evidence may support context or corroboration. It must not be treated as direct observation merely because the publication is reputable.

### Class D — Promotional, testimonial, retrospective, anonymous, or untraceable material

This includes marketing claims, undated testimonials, anonymous assertions, unsourced biographies, social posts without verifiable provenance, and retrospective claims whose basis is not inspectable.

Class D material may identify a lead or a claim requiring investigation. It cannot alone support canonization or a bounded trait derivation.

## Sufficiency Rules

1. Evidence must support the exact achievement, outcome, or professional behavior claimed; generalized praise is insufficient.
2. The achievement-to-behavior link must be explicit. An outcome does not prove which behavior produced it.
3. The behavior-to-trait interpretation must identify the observed behavior and competing explanations.
4. Context must record resources, authority, institutional support, timing, luck, access, and other non-transferable advantages when material.
5. At least one Class A or Class B source is required for a canonization candidate. Class C may corroborate or establish context but cannot substitute for a missing direct basis.
6. A single source may be sufficient for a narrow, low-uncertainty, non-disputed claim when its access and limitations are explicit. No numeric source-count rule replaces claim-specific judgment.
7. Material contradiction, disputed attribution, or causal uncertainty requires either corroboration and bounded wording or an UNRESOLVED disposition.
8. Evidence about a trait must not be generalized beyond the conditions in which the behavior was observed.
9. The proposed trait must be expressed as a testable professional or Officer behavior, not as a personality adjective or identity claim.
10. A source may support evidence without authorizing imitation, identity transfer, or attribution of the source person's credentials to a persona.

## Conflict Handling

When sources conflict:

- preserve each material claim and its provenance;
- identify whether the conflict concerns occurrence, attribution, outcome, interpretation, or context;
- do not average incompatible accounts into a synthetic fact;
- seek a stronger or more direct source where feasible;
- narrow the proposition if the conflict permits a smaller supported claim;
- otherwise mark the proposition UNRESOLVED and do not canonize the disputed derivation.

A later source does not automatically supersede an earlier source. Revision depends on the exact proposition and the evidence bearing on it.

## Dispositions

### ADMISSIBLE FOR CANON REVIEW

Use only when the exact evidence chain is inspectable, material limitations are recorded, and no unresolved contradiction defeats the bounded trait claim. This permits Hagiography or The Gesta to consider the trait; it does not canonize it.

### CONDITIONAL / BOUNDED

Use when the evidence supports a narrower trait or specified conditions but not the broader proposed interpretation. Preserve the limits in the record.

### UNRESOLVED

Use when the evidence is material but conflicting, incomplete, indirect, or insufficient to support a determinate claim. Preserve it as an open question; do not treat it as negative proof.

### REFUSED

Use when the proposed derivation relies on fame, reputation, identity, unsupported inference, untraceable material, or an impermissible whole-person or credential transfer.

## Revision and Falsification

A canon candidate must state what would revise or defeat it, including:

- a source correction that changes the exact event or outcome;
- evidence showing the behavior was misattributed;
- evidence that the result depended on an omitted non-transferable condition;
- material contradictory evidence;
- failure of the trait to produce the bounded behavior under the stated conditions;
- Pit findings showing mimicry, overextension, competence loss, evidence failure, or governance conflict.

A correction must preserve the prior record, identify the changed proposition, and invalidate dependent future use where the change is material.

## Boundaries

EC-01 does not:

- define a profession;
- decide whether a person is exemplary in full;
- establish legal, moral, medical, or psychological conclusions;
- require demographic quotas or representativeness formulas;
- create a living-person privacy policy; those safeguards remain a separate correction;
- forge, test, admit, recruit, or deploy a persona or Officer;
- grant authority or create Runtime behavior.

## Pressure-Test Questions

Before EC-01 can be considered for admission, pressure it with at least:

1. one promotional claim with no underlying evidence;
2. one strong outcome with unclear behavior causation;
3. two identified sources that conflict on attribution;
4. one narrow claim supported by a single direct source;
5. one retrospective source that exposes a material omitted context;
6. one trait that sounds desirable but cannot be expressed as testable behavior;
7. one proposed derivation that attempts to import credentials, identity, or biography;
8. one later correction that must invalidate dependent use.
