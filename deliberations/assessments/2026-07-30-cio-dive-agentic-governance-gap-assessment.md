# CIO Dive Agentic-Governance Gap Assessment

## Date

2026-07-30

## Source

See `sources/2026-07-30-cio-dive-agentic-governance-gap.md`.

## Question

What does the reported gap in enterprise agentic-AI governance add to Imperium's deliberations, what is missing, and what should be refined?

## Scope Qualification

This is a dated assessment of an external report. It does not amend an admitted decision, activate a leg, authorize implementation, or claim Runtime enforcement.

The source uses broad external language. This assessment translates only the portions relevant to Imperium's current semantic work.

## Confirmed External Findings

As reported by CIO Dive:

- agentic-AI deployment plans are advancing faster than governance maturity;
- many organizations lack governance specific to autonomous agents or apply a blanket policy;
- recommended governance includes autonomy boundaries, monitoring, action trails, cross-functional review, and measurable impact.

The underlying survey and definitions remain external claims and are not independently verified here.

## Imperium Alignment

The finding reinforces several existing Imperium distinctions:

- persona and capability are not authority;
- capability is not the same as permitted access;
- mission purpose is not permission to take every technically available action;
- activation and deployment are separate from creation and selection;
- observation is separate from execution influence;
- a record of an action is not authorization for that action;
- conceptual architecture is not operational enforcement.

The existing apparatus already provides relevant semantic locations:

- Studium for doctrine, boundaries, conduct, and evidence requirements;
- Foundry for the persona candidate specification;
- Recruitment for mission-specific transformation and deployment packaging;
- Collegium and Curia for suitability, decision, and disposition;
- Armory and Locksmith for tools and credential boundaries;
- Muster for controlled outbound orchestration;
- Execution for bounded attempts and return packaging;
- Lazaretto for return sanitation;
- Provenance and mission records for action lineage;
- Observator/Custos for independent observation and custodial reporting;
- Master Mason for Runtime operation and maintenance.

## Missing or Under-Specified Matters

### 1. Explicit autonomy classification

Every operative candidate or mission package should state its authorized autonomy class for the relevant action surface:

1. observe;
2. advise;
3. act with approval;
4. act autonomously.

This classification must be action- and mission-specific. It must not become a universal trust label attached to a persona.

The class should determine, at minimum:

- whether external action is possible;
- whether approval is required before each action or action group;
- what evidence and validation are required;
- what monitoring and sampling are required;
- what revocation or interruption behavior applies.

### 2. Capability-to-access separation

Imperium must explicitly distinguish:

- what the operative can technically do;
- what tools and data it can reach;
- what the mission authorizes;
- what the current action permits;
- what the provider or environment will accept.

A capable operative must not inherit authority merely because a tool, credential, endpoint, or provider action is available.

This sharpens the existing CTRL-003 boundary and should inform CTRL-004 capability suitability.

### 3. Proportional control burden

Governance must scale with consequence, not merely with model identity or the fact that an agent exists.

A low-consequence observer may require scoped data access and identity verification. An operative that advises may require output review, uncertainty handling, and reliance boundaries. An operative that acts with approval may require meaningful pre-action control. An autonomous operative requires the strongest constraints, anomaly detection, human sampling, interruption, and revocation.

This is a proposal for refinement, not an admitted control.

### 4. Continuous assurance, not launch approval

Approval at activation is insufficient. Authority and suitability must remain valid during the mission.

The governance envelope should identify reassessment triggers, including:

- changed tools, credentials, data scope, provider, or environment;
- changed model, prompt, persona, doctrine, or operative package;
- repeated failures, anomalies, policy conflicts, or unexpected effects;
- changed mission objective or external obligation;
- expiry, withdrawal, contest, revocation, or supersession of a prerequisite.

This connects directly to CTRL-005 and the existing invalidation/version-integrity work.

### 5. Measurable control contracts

"Good governance," "mature governance," and "responsible autonomy" are not sufficient Imperium claims.

Each future control should define observable measures such as:

- permit and denial outcomes;
- approval and reapproval latency;
- unauthorized-action attempts;
- containment and interruption success;
- anomaly detection and response;
- evidence completeness;
- return/disposition traceability;
- outcome quality against the mission objective.

This connects directly to CTRL-006. Measurement must not be confused with proof that the control is effective.

### 6. Cross-functional review without authority diffusion

Cross-functional participation is useful for identifying risk, legal obligations, operational consequences, and affected stakeholders. It must not create multiple competing decision makers or blur Curia's admitted single-decision-maker rule.

The refinement is:

- cross-functional inputs may inform suitability, doctrine, risk, and evidence requirements;
- authority remains explicitly assigned;
- disagreement must be recorded and dispositioned;
- no participant acquires execution authority merely by participating.

## Proposed Refinements

1. Add an autonomy-class field to the Foundry candidate specification or its downstream mission-specific operative package, subject to placement review.
2. Require a capability/access/authority/action matrix for consequential operative packages.
3. Tie autonomy class to differentiated evidence, approval, monitoring, sampling, interruption, revocation, and reassessment requirements.
4. Extend the deployment authorization investigation to test action-level permits and denials across all four autonomy classes.
5. Treat change-triggered reauthorization as a lifecycle property, not a one-time deployment gate.
6. Add control-measurement expectations to pending-control investigations without promoting any control to implemented status.
7. Preserve Curia's single decision-maker boundary while allowing structured cross-functional findings and objections.

## Conclusions

The external finding is substantially consistent with Imperium's direction: agentic governance cannot be a blanket policy applied after deployment. It must be proportional to the operative's authority and the consequence of its possible actions.

The most important refinement is to make autonomy a property of the authorized action and mission envelope—not a permanent property of the model, persona, or agent.

The second is to preserve the distinction:

    technical capability ≠ available access ≠ granted authority ≠ permitted action

The third is to treat governance as continuous assurance across the mission lifecycle rather than as a single approval event.

## Residual Uncertainty

- Exact autonomy-class semantics and placement remain open.
- The boundary between persona specification, mission-specific operative packaging, deployment authorization, and Execution requires pressure testing.
- Runtime mechanisms for monitoring, sampling, interruption, revocation, and measurement remain unselected and unproven.
- External governance obligations and affected-stakeholder requirements remain broader than Imperium's current deployer-level scope.
