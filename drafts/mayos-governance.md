# Mayo's Governance

## Status

Draft.

This file does not admit a new Imperium runtime layer.

It defines the higher construction discipline that governs how Imperium itself is designed, developed, reviewed, corrected, and preserved.

---

## Core Distinction

Mayo's Governance is not Imperium governance.

Imperium governance concerns what may become part of Imperium.

Mayo's Governance concerns how Imperium may be made.

Its question is:

```text
What governs the making of the thing that may later govern?
```

The current answer is:

```text
Mayo's Governance governs construction.
Imperium governance governs admission.
Imperium runtime governance is not admitted.
```

---

## Echelon

Mayo's Governance sits above Imperium as construction doctrine.

It may constrain the repository, the development environment, the operator, and any assisting LLM session.

It must not be treated as a ministry, agent, service, runtime, subsystem, or internal Imperium institution.

It is not a part inside the machine.

It is the discipline by which the machine is permitted to be made.

## Construction Doctrine

Mayo's Governance requires construction to proceed by explicit pressure, visible assumption, tested behavior, and reversible admission.

The acceptable movement is:

```text
Pressure
→ Assumption
→ Draft
→ Test
→ Evidence
→ Admission, revision, deferral, rejection, or removal
```

No step in that movement is ceremonial.

Each step exists to prevent language from hardening before behavior earns it.

## Authority Discipline

Authority must be explicit.

Mayo's Governance rejects authority soup.

A system, agent, operative, document, workflow, persona, service, or process must not silently collapse different authority types into one assumed power.

At minimum, these authorities must remain distinguishable:

- **Reporting authority** receives status, assumptions, evidence, risks, exceptions, decisions, stop-condition notices, and outcomes.
- **Command authority** may issue instructions within a defined scope.
- **Review authority** may judge outputs, behavior, evidence, or compliance.
- **Approval authority** may authorize continuation, escalation, publication, admission, or execution.

These authorities may belong to the same entity.

They may also be separated.

They must not be assumed identical merely because one authority is named.

Reporting is not obedience.

Obedience is not approval.

Approval is not review.

Review is not command.

When authority is missing, ambiguous, conflicting, unreachable, or overbroad, the condition must be surfaced as a governance gap before durable action proceeds.

## Relationship To Imperium Governance

Mayo's Governance and Imperium governance are related but not identical.

Mayo's Governance governs the development act.

Imperium governance evaluates proposed Imperium concepts.

The distinction protects Imperium from recursion.

Without this distinction, Imperium would be asked to govern its own formation before its own governing behavior is proven.

That would create premature authority.

Mayo's Governance exists to prevent that premature authority.

## Repository Effect

Mayo's Governance currently affects the repository through discipline, not automation.

It requires:

- source-of-truth inspection before modification
- small coherent commits
- visible operational context
- explicit admission tests
- deliberate naming
- reversible file movement
- evidence-based promotion or removal
- explicit authority boundaries
- clear distinction between reporting, command, review, and approval

It does not require:

- a governance engine
- a policy runtime
- a law interpreter
- a command system
- a ministry
- a database schema
- an agent hierarchy

Those would be premature unless repeated evidence proves that manual discipline is insufficient.
