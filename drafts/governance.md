# Governance Behavior

## Status

Provisional.

This file does not admit a governance layer.

It defines the smallest observable governance behavior that could justify one later.

---

## Observed Problem

Imperium needs a way to decide whether a proposed concept, file, behavior, name, or structural commitment should be admitted, rejected, deferred, revised, or removed.

Without that decision behavior, the repository can again accumulate conceptual mass faster than structural integrity.

---

## Explicit Assumptions

1. Governance begins as decision discipline, not as authority.
2. A governance layer is not yet proven necessary.
3. The first governance behavior can be expressed as an admission test.
4. The test must protect the main objective: build only what strengthens the proven pattern or the operative formed from it.
5. Anything that cannot be tested against observed behavior remains directional language, not admitted architecture.
6. Governance is not singular; different scopes of governance may emerge if different behaviors require them.

---

## Governance Scope Taxonomy

Governance may eventually operate at more than one scope.

This distinction is admitted as vocabulary pressure, not as architecture.

### Imperium Governance

Imperium governance concerns the admission, movement, revision, demotion, promotion, retirement, deletion, and restoration of concepts within Imperium itself.

Its question is:

```text
What is allowed to become part of Imperium?
```

Imperium governance is global in scope, but not yet a global layer.

This is the only governance scope currently under draft.

### Archetype Governance

Archetype governance would concern the validity, strengthening, weakening, deployment readiness, revision, and retirement of reusable agentic patterns.

Its question would be:

```text
What makes this archetype valid, fortified, unsafe, obsolete, overfit, underproven, or deployable?
```

Archetype governance remains directional until archetypes exist.

### Operative Governance

Operative governance would concern permissions, constraints, evidence duties, stop conditions, drift signals, and revision triggers for patterns under execution.

Its question would be:

```text
What is this operative allowed to do, under what conditions, with what evidence, and when must it stop?
```

Operative governance remains directional until operatives exist.

---

## Governance Behavior

For any proposed addition, Imperium governance must produce one of five outcomes:

- **admit** — the proposal has observed need, explicit assumptions, testable behavior, and removal criteria.
- **reject** — the proposal violates the main objective or introduces unjustified conceptual mass.
- **defer** — the proposal may matter later, but the current evidence is insufficient.
- **revise** — the proposal addresses a real need, but its current form is too large, too named, or too assumptive.
- **remove** — an admitted concept no longer survives contact with evidence.

---

## Admission Test

Before admitting any concept, answer:

1. What observed problem requires this?
2. What assumption does this introduce?
3. What behavior proves this is necessary?
4. What is the smallest implementation that tests it?
5. What evidence would force its removal?
6. How does this strengthen the proven pattern or the operative formed from it?
7. Which governance scope does this belong to, if any?

If these cannot be answered, the concept is not admitted.

---

## Smallest Implementation

The smallest implementation is manual review using this document.

No runtime, service, schema, command, policy engine, or workflow automation is admitted yet.

The first proof is whether this admission test improves the quality of repository decisions.

---

## Evidence Required To Admit A Layer

A governance layer may become necessary only after repeated decisions show that manual review is insufficient.

Evidence may include:

- repeated proposals needing consistent admission outcomes
- conflicting decisions that require traceable rationale
- admitted concepts needing lifecycle review
- removals needing documented cause
- operatives or archetypes requiring repeatable constraints

Until such evidence exists, governance remains behavior, not infrastructure.

---

## Removal Criteria

Remove or revise this document if:

- it produces ceremony without improving decisions
- it blocks useful proof work
- its categories fail to describe real repository decisions
- a simpler decision behavior proves sufficient
- the main objective changes
- the scope taxonomy creates premature architecture instead of useful distinction
